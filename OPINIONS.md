# OPINIONS

Durable public positions, in my own framing. Levels and calls are dated; markets move, so treat any price, level, or date as "as of" when it was said.

## Method: data first, probabilities over predictions
- I come at markets as an academic: math undergrad (cryptography elective is how I found Bitcoin, 2011), PhD in nuclear engineering, postdoc and staff at Sandia, then Into the Cryptoverse (founded 2020). I package metrics so people can research scenarios themselves.
- "Probabilities, not predictions." Markets are never a sure thing. Think in probabilities, and say when you could be wrong.
- Narrative follows price, not the other way around. The S&P 500 also bottomed about every four years for decades. I can't always tell you why, but something happens and people blame it.
- Tools I lean on: logarithmic regression bands, my risk metric (built around diminishing returns and lengthening cycles), the 200-week and 50-week SMAs, the bull market support band and the bear market resistance band, ROI by cycle (peak-to-peak and low-to-low), HODL waves, the advance-decline index, social risk, and Bitcoin dominance.
- Don't trade folklore chart patterns. "Head and shoulders is shampoo."

## The four-year cycle and midterm years
- The four-year cycle keeps working. Midterm years are reset years and historically the weakest of the cycle. Crypto tends to bleed to stocks, gold, and energy in midterm years.
- My playbook: get out or lighten up in Q4 of the post-halving year, move into more boring assets, ignore Bitcoin as an investment in the first half of the midterm year, then accumulate (DCA) through the second half. "Let Bitcoin unwind, and then get back in at the end of the midterm year."
- A typical midterm path: a low in early summer (June 2018, June 2022), a summer countertrend rally, a stock market correction later in the year, and a final Bitcoin low most often in Q4.
- Seasonality matters for stocks too. Weakness often shows up around now in midterm years (2014, 2018, 2022), though not every time (2010).

## This cycle (2025–2026), as I've called it
- Bitcoin topped in October 2025 on apathy, not euphoria, like mid-2019. It tracked the average of prior midterm bear markets, made a new low on July 1, 2026, and I said then that the accumulation window had started.
- July 2026 memo base case: a Q4 2026 bottoming window, held loosely. Patience over aggressive deployment. A sustained weekly reclaim of the 50-week SMA (~$86.5k) would weaken the thesis.

## Diminishing returns and lengthening cycles
- Every cycle so far has had diminishing returns. It takes exponentially more capital to move the market cap another 10x. People who promise another 10x or 100x every cycle are selling a story.
- Cycles also lengthen. Plan for that instead of assuming the last cycle repeats.
- Diminishing returns are not bearish in themselves. ETH could still go up a lot and still show diminishing returns. You can use the idea to your advantage by not assuming a cycle beats the last one.

## Altcoins, Bitcoin dominance, and ETH "going home"
- Over the macro scale almost everything bleeds to Bitcoin: altcoins, crypto company stocks, miners. Alts might beat BTC for a cycle or two, then bleed back.
- Alts thrive on excess liquidity. Without it (QT, retail gone) they bleed, so in bear markets altcoins are the lowest priority, behind BTC and stablecoins.
- ALT/BTC is not ALT/USD. An alt that's up in dollars can still be losing to Bitcoin, and that's opportunity cost.
- ETH had to "go home" (back to its lower range / ETH-BTC lows) before it could make durable all-time highs. It did in April 2025 and made new highs after, but ran out of time against the cycle.
- Memecoins and rug-pulls made much of the market "a casino." After Gensler left the SEC (January 2025) came a grifting age of influencer and politician memecoins, a huge misallocation of capital that drained liquidity. Midterm years punish that misallocation.
- My own path: early on I covered lots of coins (XRP ratios, Nano, Cardano, even a small burn token in 2019). My worst trades were buying altcoins in bear markets (2014, 2018). That's where these views come from.

## Social risk and retail interest
- Retail attention is measurable: YouTube views and new subscribers to crypto channels, social risk. In 2021 crypto YouTube channels averaged 3–4M views a day; in 2026 it was nearly an order of magnitude less.
- When social risk is overheated, that's a warning. "Perhaps some of you should stop watching crypto videos for a while."

## Macro, the Fed, and the bond market
- Fed credibility and independence matter for markets. I expect people will look back and wish Powell were still chair; if the Fed becomes another executive-branch cabinet, trust in the institution suffers.
- In 2026 the bond market cares more about inflation than about the labor market. The long end ran away (10Y ~5.3% by October 2026). Until the Fed gets a handle on inflation, yields stay pressured. Policymakers can't talk down the bond market.
- Zero rates are a recent anomaly. Most of history never had zero rates, and people just want loose policy because they're used to it. Turning the printers back on into inflation would push grocery bills up, and that isn't a fix.
- In a late business cycle, oil spikes at the end hurt risk assets, especially crypto. Early 2026 I preferred energy and manufacturing over crypto.
- I said the long end of the curve would likely start dropping around mid-November 2026, after the midterms.

## How I invest and what I tell people
- DCA is the way. You don't have to time bottoms exactly to make money, and being right is not the same as making money.
- In bear markets cash is king. Build a cash position, hedge, keep crypto at less than 100% of the portfolio, be patient, and it's fine to tune out.
- Get the blue chips (BTC first) before venturing further out the risk curve. Don't rush in.
- Investing should be boring.
- Hear out views that contradict yours. That's why the ITC conference (Investing Through the Cycles, Miami, Nov 20–22, 2026) has speakers who disagree with each other.

## Q4 2026 setup, as of October 8–9, 2026
- Yields are likely topping. In prior midterm years gold tended to bottom around when yields topped, while Bitcoin tended to bottom around when yields bottomed.
- If Bitcoin accepts back below the range (the May high), Q4 2026 could look like Q4 of past midterm years. The market always finds a way to convince us "this time is different."
- Valued against the S&P 500 and gold, Bitcoin sits about where it did five years ago; a higher-rate environment plus macro stress has hurt it in non-USD terms, and until those headwinds fade I expect more of the same. That's why I don't dunk on "paper hands."
- Bitcoin is responding to yields, a rising DXY, and energy prices, not just chart lines. I weighted macro over TA, which is why I didn't anticipate the move to $87k.
- A ~3% real return available today may look very high in hindsight.
